# Montreal-collisions: police-reported road collisions in Montréal, from open data

Two public series of police-reported collisions, kept with their provenance, the code that reads them, and the tables, maps and charts drawn from them. Companion to [Terrebonne-open-sources](https://github.com/OpenMobilityData/Terrebonne-open-sources) and [Terrebonne-access-requests](https://github.com/OpenMobilityData/Terrebonne-access-requests), which concern the rue de Terrebonne bike lane; the data here are city-wide and the repository is meant to outlast that question. The READMEs and notes describe and count; they do not evaluate.

**First deliverable (October 26, 2026):** the record preceding each of the 31 cyclist fatalities of 2012–2021 at the same location: [data note (PDF, 6 pp.)](derived/cyclist-fatality-locations/cyclist_fatality_locations_note_en.pdf) · [the tables in Markdown](derived/cyclist-fatality-locations/tables.md) · [one row per fatality (CSV)](derived/cyclist-fatality-locations/cyclist_fatality_locations_2012-2021.csv) · [rue de Terrebonne on the same scale (CSV)](derived/cyclist-fatality-locations/terrebonne_same_scale_2012-2021.csv) · [summaries](derived/cyclist-fatality-locations/summary_earlier_fatal.csv), [by severity and mode](derived/cyclist-fatality-locations/summary_earlier_record.csv).

The two series are never merged: different years, territory and reporting. Each figure names its series and its years.

## Definitions (SPVM series, `scripts/spvm.py`)

- **Severity** is the collision's own `GRAVITE`: *Mortel* (fatal), *Grave* (severe injury), *Léger* (light injury), and the two property-damage classes (*Dommages matériels seulement*, *… inférieurs au seuil de rapportage*) merged as *damage*. Fatal collisions: 263, the same rows as `NB_MORTS > 0`.
- **Mode.** A collision *involves a bicycle* when `nb_bicyclette > 0` or `NB_VICTIMES_VELO > 0` (8,685); *a pedestrian* when `NB_VICTIMES_PIETON > 0` (10,722); *a motorcycle* when `nb_motocyclette > 0` or `NB_VICTIMES_MOTO > 0` (2,947); *motor vehicles only* otherwise (no pedestrian, bicycle or motorcycle involved, whatever the severity). A collision may be in more than one of the first three.
- **Street key** for « same street name » matches: upper case, accents and hyphens removed, generic words dropped (rue, av, boul, ch, O, E, face, opp…), *St* expanded to *Saint*, *N-D* to *Notre Dame*, tokens sorted. A crude match, kept beside the distance definitions.
- **Distances** are planar, on the City's `LOC_X`/`LOC_Y` (MTM zone 8, NAD83); `spvm.mtm8()` projects a latitude/longitude to the same system (checked against the file's own pairs: within 5 cm).
- **Extents** of the earlier figures (latitude/longitude boxes, strict bounds): NDG box 45.4390–45.4875 N, 73.7005–73.5808 W (50.3 km²); H4C box 45.4645–45.4850 N, 73.5870–73.5640 W (4.1 km²); the Terrebonne & Somerled corridor is a street-name selection.

## Reconciliation with the 2024 figures (`scripts/reproduce_2024.py` → `derived/tables/reproduce_2024.txt`)

A set of maps and charts drawn from the same CSV in 2024 with a MATLAB script (retired; archived outside this repository) printed these totals: 8,697 cyclist collisions (31 fatal, 280 severe, 6,298 light, 2,088 damage), 218,685 collisions in all, 10,814 pedestrian and 2,963 motorcycle collisions, 22,101 in the NDG box, 3,736 in the H4C box, 516 on Terrebonne and Somerled. Every one of these is reproduced exactly as the **sum of overlapping classes**: that script built each severity class from several conditions (for instance, a collision with a lightly injured cyclist and a severely injured other person fell in both the light and the severe class), and its legend totals added the classes. Counted as distinct rows the figures are 8,685 (31 / 279 / 6,287 / 2,088), 218,272, 10,722, 2,947, 22,062, 3,733 and 515. The 2nd-vehicle counts of the 2024 cyclist map match (cars and light trucks 7,617; heavy trucks 257; buses 130; motorcycles 44; construction equipment 15; other 186) except taxis, printed as 2 from a `> 1` test where `> 0` gives 414. The area printed on each 2024 map is the box's diagonal multiplied by its width, not its area (NDG printed 100.6 km², rectangle 50.3; Montréal printed 1,942.3, bounding rectangle 1,273.6). This repository reports distinct collisions and rectangle areas.

## Maps and charts (`scripts/maps.py`, `scripts/charts.py`, `scripts/basemap.py`; October 26, 2026)

Figures are in `derived/figures/maps/` and `derived/figures/charts/`, PNG and SVG (the island maps PNG only: 218,000 points), English and French (`_en`, `_fr`). Colours follow a validated palette (the `dataviz` skill's reference palette, fixed per class, never cycled); marks are thin, every legend carries the count per class and the total, every title names the extent, its area as a rectangle and the period.

**Maps** (dark basemap: Esri « World Dark Gray Base », credited on each map, tiles fetched into a cache outside the repository and not redistributed). Three extents: `island` (every collision with coordinates), `ndg` (the NDG box above), `corridor` (collisions within 25 m of the Terrebonne lane axis from Belmore to Girouard, the axis dashed, with the street-name selection printed beside as the comparison); the 2024 « H4C box » (Saint-Henri, Little Burgundy, Pointe-Saint-Charles) stays in the loader for reproduction but is not drawn by default. Seven views, each for bicycles and for pedestrians where it applies: `severity` (every collision; shape = road users involved, with pedestrian ▽, bicycle □, motorcycle ◇, motor vehicles only ○, the last meaning no pedestrian, bicycle or motorcycle involved, whatever the severity; colour and size = the collision's severity, fatal drawn last in white with a ring); `bike_severity`, `ped_severity` and `vru_severity` (the severity view restricted to collisions involving a bicycle, a pedestrian victim, or either, at every extent); `bike_vehicle` and `ped_vehicle` (collisions involving a bicycle, or with a pedestrian victim, coloured by the other vehicle recorded, in this order of precedence: heavy truck or tractor-trailer, bus or minibus, car or light truck, other or none recorded); `bike_fatal` and `ped_fatal` (island: the 31 collisions in which a cyclist was killed, numbered in date order with a three-column key under the map, and the 156 in which a pedestrian was killed, listed in `derived/tables/{bike,ped}_fatalities_2012-2021.csv`; coloured by the other vehicle); `bike` and `ped` (corridor: every collision involving a bicycle, 15, or with a pedestrian victim, 11, within 25 m of the lane axis or with Terrebonne in a street field, each labelled with its date and coloured by severity); `vru_vehicle`, `vru_fatal` and `vru`, the same three views for the two together (a bicycle involved or a pedestrian victim: 19,403 collisions, 187 fatal, 26 on the corridor). Collisions between a bicycle and a pedestrian are not covered by this file: it holds the police accident reports for road vehicles, and in the SAAQ's coding a bicycle is not a road vehicle (the dictionary's list runs from the car to the snowmobile without it), so a bicycle striking a pedestrian with no motor vehicle involved is outside the reporting regime. The 4 rows of 2012–2021 in which a bicycle and a pedestrian both appear (all light injury, 3 with a motor vehicle also involved, none in the NDG box or on Terrebonne) are incidental, and the file says nothing about how often such collisions occur. Collisions recorded at one point are spread on a small spiral so that every marker is visible, the most severe outermost, and a note on each map says so (not on the island maps). For the dense extents (island and NDG box) a density family, `heat`, `bike_heat`, `ped_heat`, `vru_heat`: each collision spread over a Gaussian kernel (σ = 250 m on the island, 100 m in the box) on a 50 m or 20 m raster, expressed as collisions per km² on a linear single-hue ramp with a colour bar (the top 0.5 % of the raster is clipped to the bar's end); collisions recorded at one intersection add up there, so the hot spots are the intersections with the longest records. Two further density figures by `scripts/heatmaps.py` (island and NDG box): `heat_severity`, three panels side by side with the same kernel and their own colour bars (property damage only, light injury, severe injury or fatal), and `heat_bivariate`, one map in which opacity follows the density of all collisions and the hue the smoothed share of collisions with an injury, shrunk toward the extent's overall share with a prior of 20 collisions (blue below the average share, light neutral at it, orange above; the hue range runs from the 10th to the 90th percentile of the share over the populated cells; a 3 × 3 legend with the three shares printed).

**Charts.** `saaq_montreal_severity` and `saaq_montreal_type`: collisions per year in the SAAQ's Montréal region 2011–2022 as small multiples (one panel per class, each from zero: the classes differ by two orders of magnitude, so one shared axis would flatten the injury series); `spvm_<island|ndg>_modes`: collisions per year involving a pedestrian victim, a bicycle or a motorcycle, SPVM 2012–2021, legend with the ten-year totals; `spvm_<island|ndg>_shares`: the same as a share of all collisions, with a fitted linear trend (dotted) and the last value labelled. Yearly tables behind them: `derived/tables/spvm_<extent>_modes_yearly_2012-2021.csv`, `saaq_montreal_yearly_2011-2022.csv`.

## Does the earlier record at a location predict a later death? (`scripts/location_record_test.py`, October 26, 2026)

The cyclist-fatality note shows that an earlier fatal collision at the same place was not a precursor. This test asks the other question: whether the volume of the earlier record was. Units are 100 m grid cells on the City's coordinates (200 m as a check); the record of 2012–2016 is compared with the deaths of 2017–2021 (2012–2015 / 2016–2021 as a check). Outputs in `derived/location-record-and-later-deaths/`: the percentile of each death site's earlier record among all cells with a record (`death_sites_percentiles.csv`), deaths per 100 cells by decile of the earlier injury-collision count (`deaths_by_decile.csv`, chart `deaths_by_decile_{en,fr}`), the share of deaths caught by the top k % of cells (`deaths_caught_by_top_cells.csv`), the Terrebonne cells on the same scale (`terrebonne_cells_percentiles.csv`), and a data note. Headline: of the 17,593 cells with a record in 2012–2016, the top tenth by injury collisions held 6 of the 13 cyclist deaths and 53 of the 87 pedestrian deaths of 2017–2021; the bottom seven tenths held 1 and 13. Death sites sit around the 90th percentile of the earlier record. No exposure measure enters: the record stands in part for traffic volume.

## A crude point normalization: collisions against the City's intersection counts (`scripts/point_rates.py`, October 27, 2026)

The City's open data « Comptages des véhicules, cyclistes et piétons aux intersections » holds one-day counts at Terrebonne's signalized crossings: Girouard (three days in November 2018, three in May 2019, seven in September 2023), Draper (February 2018), Côte-Saint-Luc (April 2015), and partial days at Girouard and Cavendish (August and September 2009), Grand (2019), Madison (June and October 2021) and Cavendish (2023). All thirteen of the portal's resources were checked on October 27, 2026 (the 2008–2010, 2020–2022 and 2023–2025 files as currently served; the « 3 dernières années » and 2026–2028 files hold no Terrebonne count). `derived/tables/terrebonne_intersection_counts_by_day.csv` gives each count-day's bicycles, pedestrians and motor vehicles (older files record bicycles by approach, the 2023 file by movement; the script takes whichever is filled) and the hours covered. `terrebonne_point_rates_2012-2021.csv` sets the police-reported collisions of 2012–2021 within 100 m of each fully counted intersection against the mean daily crossings of its full count-days, as collisions per million crossings with exact Poisson intervals, and beside them the rate at the lowest and the highest daily count observed. The exposure is the weak link: at Girouard the daily bicycle count runs from 1 (November 2018) to 1,128 (September 2023), so the rate's interval from exposure is far wider than its Poisson interval, and a one-day count is not a year's exposure. The table is kept as the first exposure-aware figure for the street, with these limits, not as a measure of risk. `derived/figures/charts/girouard_count_days_{en,fr}.png` (`girouard_counts_chart.py`, beside it) charts the thirteen full count-days at Girouard, and `point_rates_{en,fr}.png` (`point_rates_chart.py`) the rates themselves on a log axis, each with its Poisson interval and its exposure band, with rue Sherbrooke's counted intersections inside the NDG box (Cavendish, Décarie, Addington, Girouard, Walkley, Montclair) as the comparison; a full count-day on which a mode was not counted (zero for that mode) is left out for that mode; bicycles from single digits in November 2018 to 1,128 on a September weekday in 2023, pedestrians 1,444–2,200, motor vehicles 8,100–12,300, all before the 2024 lane.
