# Cycling injuries and collisions with motor vehicles as a public-health question: a review

**Status:** preliminary results, not peer reviewed (version v2026-11-26-12-09-41). **Author:** Rick Hoge, PhD. **Language:** English (the summary graphics also exist in French).

An umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses, with a structured review of Canadian primary studies and of official statistics. It asks how injuries and deaths of cyclists, and the part that involves a motor vehicle, rank among public-health considerations, and ends with an assessment against six criteria fixed before the search. It makes no recommendation. It is a review of published evidence and official statistics, not medical or public-health advice.

## How it was made, in brief

- **Protocol first.** The questions, the six criteria, the sources, the search strings and the eligibility rules were written and committed before the search (November 15, 2026). Two amendments of November 26, 2026 are described in the review's section on deviations.
- **Search and screening.** PubMed, Europe PMC and OpenAlex; every record screened twice, independently, by AI agents given only the written criteria; disagreements read a third time.
- **The passage rule.** Every figure quoted from a study or from an official source carries a verbatim passage that a program found in the stored text of that source; every number typed in the review is checked against its passage when the document is built.
- **The author's checks.** Two random samples judged by the author against the abstracts and passages: 30 of 30 extracted figures and 39 of 40 screening decisions in the first; 15 of 15 figures and 15 of 15 official-statistics rows in the second. The one disagreement and what followed from it are in the review's last appendix.
- **Use of AI.** The searches, the screening, the extraction and the drafting were carried out by an AI system (Claude, Anthropic) under the author's direction; the review states this and its limits.

## What is not here

The working files (protocol, search log, screening decisions, extraction cards, tables of official statistics, build programs) are in the author's working repository, which is private. Article texts and saved official documents are not redistributed.

## How to read the figures on children

The children's graphics give counts, not risk: how much children cycle in traffic was not measured. Each graphic says so inside the chart, and the companion panel sets out what the counts show, what they cannot show, and what other studies add.
